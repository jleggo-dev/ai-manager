# Cadence — Brand Identity

*Working draft v1 — for redlining. Decisions marked ⚑ are open.*

---

## 1. What Cadence is

Cadence is a conversational AI lifestyle coach. It builds your plan around your actual life — and rebuilds it when your life changes.

**Positioning statement:** Cadence is the coach that plans around your life instead of demanding your life plan around it.

**Positioning (long form):**

> For **busy people** who **struggle to build consistent habits around a demanding schedule**, **Cadence** is an **AI lifestyle coach** that **builds a structured plan to advance their goals**. Unlike **Noom, MyFitnessPal, Headspace, or Calm**, Cadence **adapts your routine around your life and gives honest feedback to keep you progressing**.

Note the two differentiators in the final clause: *adapts around your life* (the thesis) and *honest feedback* (the voice — see §6, "warm but not soft"). Both must stay true in every surface or the positioning is a lie.

Every competitor hands you a program and measures your compliance. When life breaks the program, their answer is guilt — the streak dies, the ring stays open. Cadence's answer is: the plan changes. Disrupted mode is not a feature. It is the thesis.

## 2. Mission

Help busy people build habits that hold up when life doesn't.

## 3. Audience

People 30–60 with kids, demanding jobs, or both — busy people on the go who are struggling to maintain or rebuild consistent habits. They have the disposable income to pay for a coach, gym, or yoga studio, and many already do. We launch on iPhone because that's where this audience and this price point live; expansion follows once the base is built there. The platform is a starting point, not an identity.

Two implications that govern everything downstream:

1. **These are the people whose Tuesday gets destroyed** — sick kid, red-eye, deadline. We are not selling habit formation to them; everyone sells that. We are selling habits that hold through disruption.
2. **They pay coach money, so we look coach-grade.** The brand must sit comfortably next to an Oura ring and a boutique studio membership — never next to a children's game. This is the hard line under "less cartoony."

Both athletes and yogis. Nothing in the brand may code hard toward athletic intensity (tempo meters, "crush it") or toward soft wellness (lotuses, whisper-voice). The palette, imagery, and copy stay in the neutral warm middle.

## 4. Competitive position

Two axes: **rigid program ↔ adapts to you** and **tool ↔ relationship**.

| | Rigid | Adaptive |
|---|---|---|
| **Tool** | Streaks, Habitica, trackers | Whoop/Oura (data, no coaching) |
| **Relationship** | Fabulous, Noom (program + coaching theater) | **Cadence** · Future, human trainers ($150–200/mo, scheduling friction) |

**Cadence's quadrant is the adaptive relationship — at software prices, always in your pocket.** Our real competitor for this audience is not a free habit app; it's the human coach they can't schedule. Headspace and Calm are content libraries, not relationships — different aisle.

What Duolingo is to us: a reference for accessibility and craft, **not** a competitor and **not** an engagement model. We explicitly reject its mechanics (see §8).

## 5. Tagline system

One line doesn't carry a brand; a small system does. **Rule for this stage: nobody knows what Cadence is yet, so every line must say what Cadence does.** Evocative lines are a luxury earned after awareness exists — Nike can say "Just do it" because you already know what Nike sells.

| Role | Line | Where it lives |
|---|---|---|
| Primary | **Habits built around your schedule.** | Website hero, App Store page, decks |
| Coach's sign-off | **Find your cadence.** | Onboarding, email closers, the coach's own voice |
| Functional subtitle | Build better habits | App Store subtitle (search clarity). "Habits around your schedule" also fits the 30-char limit if we'd rather put the differentiator into search. |

Retired, with reasons (so they don't come back):

- *"Change is inevitable. Your habits aren't."* — a threat; fear-of-loss is the competitor mechanic we reject.
- *"Life changes. Cadence adapts."* and *"Change is inevitable. So is sunrise."* — say nothing about what Cadence does. The sunrise sentiment lives in the mark, where it belongs.

The campaign-line slot is intentionally empty until there's awareness to trade on.

## 6. Voice & tone

The product is a conversation, so the coach's voice **is** the brand — more than any visual. These rules are simultaneously brand guidelines and system-prompt material for every generated message, including notifications.

Defined as tensions:

- **Warm but not soft.** It cares about you *and* holds you to things.
- **Direct but never harsh.** Short sentences. No hedging, no exclamation-point cheerleading, no emoji pep.
- **Knowing but not surveilling.** It remembers your life ("how'd the trip go?") without reciting your data back at you. Data appears only when it's actionable right now.
- **Calm but not passive.** Steady and unhurried, but always moves you forward with a concrete next step.
- **Honest about gaps.** Never pretends the missed week didn't happen; never dwells on it either.
- **Matched to your register.** The coach mirrors the user's temperament — measured with the yogi, fired up with the Spartan racer. Tone-matching is the adaptive thesis applied to voice. What never varies: honesty, no shame, no manufactured urgency, rest is never a failure. (Marketing and generic surfaces address strangers, so they stay in the neutral warm middle; register-matching only exists inside the relationship.)

### The return moment (the defining interaction)

Our users' defining experience is falling off and coming back. The return must carry zero shame.

> ✗ "You lost your 12-day streak! 😢 Don't give up!"
> ✓ "Welcome back. Rough two weeks? Let's rebuild from where you actually are — what does this week look like?"

### Intensity is earned, and it points backward

Celebration may be loud; instruction is always calm. The coach praises what you *did* at full volume and directs what you'll *do* with level-headed care:

> ✓ "You killed it on that run yesterday — the 8 hours of sleep probably helped. Today you're fasted and short on sleep, so take it easy: zone 2 only."
> ✓ "Wow — you finished that 50km Spartan in record time. You're a beast."

Both are on-brand: recognition of something real that already happened, matched to an athlete's register. The identical vocabulary fails when intensity is manufactured and pointed forward as a demand:

> ✗ "Be a beast today! Crush your workout! 💪"

The drill sergeant isn't defined by loud words. He's defined by using intensity as pressure, on everyone, regardless of what they did. The coach celebrates loudly when it's earned and never shouts to extract compliance.

### Notifications are the coach speaking

The lock screen is our largest brand surface — seen 10–20× more often than the app is opened. Notifications are therefore **generated by the coach with real context**, never pre-baked deterministic nudges. Real, honest, achievable:

> ✓ "You've got 20 minutes between the school run and your 9:00. That's enough for the short session. Want it?"
> ✓ "It's 8pm and you're 2,000 steps short of your 10K. Got time for a jaunt around the block?"

Rules for the nudge generator:

1. Every nudge is **actionable within the next hour** — otherwise it's nagging, not coaching.
2. Data is cited only in service of the action, never as a scoreboard.
3. **Silence is a coaching move.** The coach knows when not to speak: late nights, declared rest days, mid-disruption. Frequency-capped. A coach who talks constantly is an app; one who speaks at the right moment is a coach.
4. No loss-framing, ever. Nothing is "at risk," "about to break," or "lost."

## 7. Emotions we evoke

- **Relief** — "finally, something that adjusts."
- **Momentum, in your register** — quiet steadiness for one user, earned fire for another. The app never manufactures adrenaline; the sport supplies it, and the coach reflects and protects it.
- **Earned pride** — "I'm finally crushing it." The user's words, in the user's register; the coach reflects them back, never scripts them.
- **Being known** — it remembers my life without creeping me out.
- **Unashamed return** — the emotion competitors weaponize is the one we make safe. Design everything around this.

## 8. What we are not

- **Not a drill sergeant.** Intensity is never manufactured, never generic, never used as pressure. Earned, backward-looking intensity is on-brand (§6): "You're a beast" after a record race is recognition; "Be a beast today!" is a demand.
- **Not a game.** Progress is visible without points, confetti, or XP.
- **Not a guilt machine.** No loss-framing, no streak-shame, no sad mascots. If a pattern's justification is "it worked for Duolingo," that is the reason to reject it.
- **Not clinical.** Coach, not clinician. No diagnoses, no medical claims, no lab-report tone.
- **Not hustle culture.** Rest is part of the plan, not a failure state. Intense effort is celebrated; it is never the price of the coach's approval.
- **Not spa wellness.** No lotuses, no lavender gradients, no whisper-voice.
- **Not childish.** Friendly through warmth and craft, never through bubbliness.

## 9. Visual direction

### Mark: Metronome Split

A geometric uppercase **C** split on a sharp **45° diagonal** — terracotta day above, dusk night below — with the cut offset so it exits through the letter's aperture. Chosen because it carries both halves of the brand at once: **rhythm** (the metronome angle) and **a full day** (the colour split), without relying on the contested sunrise trope.

- Flat solid fills, two layers (day terracotta / night dusk), no gradients or baked lighting — required for the iOS Icon Composer pipeline (Default / Dark / Mono from one layered source) and correct for Liquid Glass, where the system renders depth itself.
- Must survive 40 px and the mono test. The diagonal cut is geometry, so it holds when colour collapses; the day/night reading is colour-dependent and softens in mono.
- **App icon grounds:** Default (regular light home screen) sits on pure white `#FFFFFF`. Dark sits on charcoal ground `#1C1A17`. Product cream `#F4EFE4` is an in-app / brand-ground colour, not the Default icon plate.
- **Known risk:** a metronome is a device that keeps time *for* you; Cadence is meant to bend to the user's rhythm. The angle must read as tempo and momentum, never as drill-sergeant precision. Pairing with warm voice and cream product surfaces is what keeps it coach-grade.
- Lineup gate still applies: at 60 px beside Rise, Headspace, Calm, Noom, and Apple Fitness as a home-screen neighbour. If a stranger can't pick Cadence out, revisit.

### The warmth ladder

The sun's warmth is an engagement-state system used **inside the product only** (header, progress, coach surfaces) — never the app icon, which stays fixed:

| State | Meaning | Value (light mode) |
|---|---|---|
| Dormant | away a while; plan banked down | `#F5C4B3` |
| Returning | rebuilding | `#F0997B` |
| In rhythm | the week is holding | `#D85A30` |

The ladder is the ember→fire idea translated to light: it dims and rebuilds, it is never lost. It must never be presented as a score or a streak.

### Palette

**Organizing idea: the palette is one day.** Dawn sun, daylight cream, pine horizon, dusk blue, night charcoal. This isn't decoration — a day is the smallest unit of cadence, and it gives us a principled way to extend the palette: new accents come from times of day, not from a color wheel.

Core:

| Role | Light | Dark |
|---|---|---|
| Sun / primary accent | `#D85A30` | `#F0997B` |
| Horizon / secondary | `#0F6E56` | `#5DCAA5` |
| Dusk / night accent — sleep, evening wind-down, rest | `#3E5C76` | `#9DBBD4` |
| Ground / background (in-app) | `#F4EFE4` | `#1C1A17` |
| App icon plate (Default / Dark) | `#FFFFFF` | `#1C1A17` |

The dusk blue is deliberately desaturated slate, not tech blue — it has to feel like sky at 9pm, sit calmly next to terracotta, and never read as a link color.

Semantic (starting values — validate contrast during the token pass):

| Role | Light | Dark | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Success | `#2E9E6B` | `#6FCF9A` | Brighter leaf, so horizon green stays structural, not semantic |
| Warning | `#C98A2D` | `#E6B34D` | Ochre — stays in the earth family |
| Error | `#9E2B25` | `#E0847A` | Must sit clearly darker and cooler than the sun coral |

**Hard rule:** the sun coral is never used for errors or destructive actions. It lives too close to red; if coral means both "warmth" and "danger," the brand's central symbol becomes ambiguous. Error is always the deeper brick.

Warm neutrals ramp (cream → charcoal): `#F4EFE4` · `#E9E2D4` · `#CFC7B6` · `#9B9384` · `#6B6458` · `#3D3B37` · `#1C1A17`

The sun remains the **only** hot accent. Restraint in the palette is what buys us both athletes and yogis, and what separates us from wellness-lavender and sport-neon alike. All values above are starting points to test on-screen, not gospel.

**Known collision:** Headspace (orange dot) and Noom (coral-orange) — two of the four competitors in §1 — own bright warm orange. What differentiates us is *material*, not hue: theirs is loud digital candy-orange; ours is desaturated clay, earthed by pine and cream. It's the combination that's ownable, never the coral alone. **Discipline: the terracotta never drifts brighter or more saturated in any asset** — every step toward "energetic app orange" is a step toward reading as a Headspace cousin.

**Celebration accent (extension path):** the palette handles steady-state and return; it has no register for peak moments ("you're a beast"). Per the one-day rule, the unused hour is noon — a bright noon-gold (test around `#F0A83C`) reserved as a rare, *earned* flash for genuine peaks. Rare is load-bearing: if it appears weekly, it's confetti, and §8 has a rule about confetti.

**Execution warning:** terracotta + pine tips autumnal-rustic (farmhouse craft) the moment it meets hand-drawn texture or ornament. Flat geometry and disciplined type are what hold this palette in premium territory.

### Typography

**Chosen (2026-08-07): Plus Jakarta Sans** — the Cadence typeface. Geometric-humanist, warm, slightly distinctive; free on Google Fonts. Use it for the wordmark, display/marketing, in-app headers, and web/Android UI.

Friendly through proportion and openness, **not** through bubbly rounding (rounding is how "cartoony" gets back in). Generous sizes, calm hierarchy, coach-message text set for comfortable reading.

| Face | Role |
|---|---|
| **Plus Jakarta Sans** | Brand typeface — wordmark, display, headers, web/Android UI |
| **SF Pro** (system) | Native iOS chrome + chat body only — Dynamic Type / optical sizes for the conversation surface (audience is 30–60; reading comfort beats brand distinctiveness in long chat) |

Retired from the shortlist (do not revive without a new decision): General Sans, Söhne, Fraunces as in-app faces. Fraunces may still appear as an optional marketing-only editorial accent, never inside the product.

**Avoid:** fully rounded faces (Nunito, Quicksand, Baloo, Comfortaa) — that's the door cartoony walks back through. Inter is acceptable as a last-resort web/Android body fallback but must never replace Plus Jakarta Sans at display sizes.

### Imagery

Coach and campaign imagery: **stylized human illustration** — realistic proportions, warmth and expression, clearly drawn, never photoreal (photoreal AI humans read as fake, imply a real person, and are hardest to keep consistent). Activity mix deliberately spans running, stretching, walking, cooking, sleeping — never all-sport, never all-zen. Produced via a Scenario model trained on a commissioned seed set in this style. ⚑ Seed set to be commissioned.

B2B exception: a real gym's real coach, with consent, may appear as themselves.

### Motion

Dawn-like: slow warms, gentle rises, nothing that bounces or squashes. Motion is the easiest place for cartoony to sneak back in.

## 10. Consistency mandate

Component-level visual inconsistency is a brand problem before it is a design problem. A design-token pass (type scale, color ramp, spacing, radius, elevation) is the highest-leverage visual fix and precedes any new asset production.
